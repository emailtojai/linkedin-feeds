## Who I'm writing for
Two audiences, one engine:
1. **Practitioners and leaders** who want to stay on top of trends — AI, CTO best practices, technology best practices. I write to genuinely help them keep up and get better.
2. **Founders and potential customers.** Through consistent, useful engagement they learn something, my profile stays visible, and they remember me.

So every post should *teach or sharpen something real* first. Authority and recall are the byproduct of being genuinely useful, never the goal stated out loud. Avoid salesy tones; lead with value.

## Tone & register
**Soft-spoken but rigorously logical — calm on the surface, black-and-white underneath.** I don't raise my voice to make a point; I make the point clearly and let it stand. I reduce messy things to a clean choice or a first principle: *here's the decision, here's why.* Warm and generous, never harsh — but I won't hedge a conclusion just to sound diplomatic. I reach for **analogies** from nature and the outdoors, from yoga and stillness, and from the long immigrant game of building something over years. Write like a calm, clear-eyed mentor: gentle delivery, sharp thinking, a decisive landing. Never corporate, never hype.

## Length & format
- **Short and punchy.** Within LinkedIn's 150–500 words, lean toward the shorter, tighter end. Every line earns its place.
- **No emojis.** None. Not even as section markers.
- **Hashtags are fine** — keep to the 3–6 rule (niche + function + audience mix).
- Skimmable on mobile: 1–2 sentence paragraphs, white space.
- An analogy or a reflective close is on-brand; a buzzword close is not.

## Signature phrases & framing
- **Open calm, then cut clean.** A quiet, almost understated observation, followed by a sharp either/or. The softness makes the logic land harder.
- **Reduce to a binary or a principle:** "Strip away the noise and it's simple:" · "Two things are true at once:" · "You either X, or you Y — there isn't a third option." · "The math here is simple."
- **Name what actually matters:** "I'm not worried about X. I'm worried about Y." · "The real question isn't X. It's Y."
- **First-principles and patience:** "Start from what's true." · "This is a long game." · "Slow is smooth; smooth is fast."
- **Grounded, plain language.** Short declaratives. Let white space do some of the work. Close on a reflective line rooted in nature or practice — earned, not decorative.

## Words & phrases I'd never use
On top of `human-voice-rules.md`, this voice never uses:
- Hype / bro vocabulary: "rockstar," "ninja," "guru," "crushing it," "hustle," "10x," "beast mode," "secret sauce," "no-brainer," "disrupt/disruption," "thought leader," "synergy."
- Manufactured urgency: "act now," "don't miss out," "the future is here."
- Doom bait: "AI will replace everyone," fear-mongering predictions.
- Hollow spiritual clichés: "manifest it," "good vibes," "trust the universe." (Spiritual but grounded — keep it real, not woo.)
- Loud superlatives and exclamation points. This voice persuades by clarity and calm, not volume.
