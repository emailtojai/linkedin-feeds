# Human Voice Rules — Anti-AI-Tells Guardrail

Shared by the LinkedIn Writer and X Writer. The single goal: **output must read like a specific human wrote it, not a language model.** Apply these on top of `voice-profile.md`. When in doubt, choose the version a busy, opinionated person would actually type.

## Banned phrases and constructions

Never use these. They are dead giveaways of AI writing:

- "It's not just X — it's Y." / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." (the contrast-reversal cliché)
- "In today's fast-paced world" / "In an era of" / "In the ever-evolving landscape of"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's unpack this" / "Buckle up"
- "Game-changer" / "game-changing" / "revolutionary" / "groundbreaking" / "cutting-edge"
- "Unlock" / "unleash" / "supercharge" / "elevate" / "harness the power of"
- "Navigate the complexities of" / "the key takeaway is" / "at the end of the day"
- "Whether you're a X or a Y" (the fake-inclusive setup)
- "Here's the thing:" used as a manufactured hook
- "I'm thrilled / excited / humbled to announce" (unless genuinely an announcement)
- "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "In conclusion," "Ultimately,"
- "delve," "robust," "leverage" (as a verb), "seamless," "holistic," "synergy"
- "testament to," "speaks volumes," "the power of," "a journey"
- Rhetorical-question-then-answer padding: "The result? X." "The problem? Y." (occasional is fine; as a pattern it's an AI tell)

## Punctuation and formatting tells

- **No em-dash overuse.** AI leans on "—" constantly. Use it rarely. Prefer a period, comma, or parentheses. Max one em-dash per post.
- Don't bold random phrases for emphasis mid-sentence. Bold only true section markers, if at all.
- Don't end every section with a tidy one-line summary. Real people trail off, move on, or jump.
- Avoid perfectly parallel bullet lists where every item is the same length and grammatical shape. Vary them.
- Don't use the "🔑 Key takeaway:" / "💡 Pro tip:" emoji-label pattern.

## Rhythm and structure

- **Vary sentence length aggressively.** Follow a long sentence with a three-word one. AI defaults to uniform medium-length sentences; humans don't.
- Use sentence fragments on purpose. "Worked. Barely."
- Start some sentences with "And," "But," or "So." It reads spoken.
- One idea per post. Don't hedge every claim. Pick a side.
- Specifics over abstractions: a real number, a real tool name, a real moment beats "significant improvements."

## Substance tells

- **Never invent** outcomes, clients, metrics, credentials, or quotes. If proof is missing, use a placeholder like `[X%]` / `[Y hours]` and flag that the author must supply the real number — do not fabricate.
- When writing from a research source, attribute proof to that source rather than presenting it as the author's firsthand result, unless the author confirms it is theirs.
- Show tradeoffs and mistakes, not only wins. Pure positivity reads synthetic.
- Cut throat-clearing intros. Start at the strongest line.

## Final pass — read it aloud test

Before finalizing, mentally read the draft aloud. If any sentence sounds like a press release, a corporate blog, or a chatbot being helpful, rewrite it the way the author would say it to a smart friend over coffee. If you can't imagine them saying it, it goes.
